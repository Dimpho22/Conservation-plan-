Indri Lemur Conservation Planning

Project Overview

This project develops a conceptual conservation plan for the indri lemur (Indri indri) in Madagascar’s Makira Forest. It applies the Open Standards for the Practice of Conservation framework to identify conservation priorities, assess threats, and evaluate strategies aimed at protecting the species and its habitat.

Project Objectives

* Define the conservation scope and long-term vision.
* Identify conservation targets and key ecological attributes (KEAs).
* Assess direct and indirect threats to the indri and its habitat.
* Evaluate potential conservation strategies based on impact and feasibility.
* Develop a conceptual model linking threats, strategies, and desired conservation results.

Methodology

The project follows a structured conservation-planning approach:

1. Scope and vision: Define the geographic focus and desired conservation outcome.
2. Conservation targets: Identify ecological and population factors relevant to indri conservation.
3. Threat assessment: Evaluate threats such as hunting, deforestation, mining, forest fires, and habitat destruction.
4. Strategy evaluation: Consider interventions including forest patrols, education, habitat protection, and conservation awareness.
5. Conceptual modelling: Organise the relationships between conservation targets, threats, and proposed strategies into a visual model.

Tools and Frameworks

* Microsoft PowerPoint — presentation and visual communication.
* Open Standards for the Practice of Conservation — conservation planning framework.
* Conceptual modelling — mapping relationships between conservation targets, threats, and strategies.

Project Context

This project was completed as an academic group assignment. The proposed strategies and assessments represent the group’s conservation-planning work and are not presented as implemented or experimentally validated interventions.
